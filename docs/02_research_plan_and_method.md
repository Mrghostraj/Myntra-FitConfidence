# Research Plan & Method

## Research questions
1. How do shoppers choose a T-Shirt size today?
2. How often do fit mismatches occur in this small sample?
3. What information do shoppers use when uncertain?
4. Which fit guidance do respondents say would be useful?
5. Which profile inputs are respondents comfortable providing?
6. What would make a recommendation trustworthy?

## Method
- Exploratory Google Forms survey, 18 questions.
- Responses collected via convenience sampling.
- Core analysis restricted to respondents indicating a T-Shirt purchase online in the previous six months (n=14).
- Multiple-select questions counted per selected option; percentages can exceed 100% across options.
- Open-text responses reviewed thematically.
- Survey analytics screenshots and response workbook are source artifacts held by project author; avoid committing identifiable timestamps/emails or raw personal data.

## Limitations
- Small sample (n=14 for eligible core analysis).
- Convenience sample, likely not representative.
- Survey responses are self-reported and subject to recall/social desirability bias.
- Three respondents in the eligible group were below 18, although target survey was meant for adult participants. Treat this as a sample eligibility deviation; exclude minors in future research or obtain suitable consent.
- Stated feature usefulness does not prove adoption, recommendation accuracy, conversion uplift, or return reduction.
- No controlled experiment or Myntra internal data was available.

## Follow-up research
- Recruit a larger, more diverse adult sample.
- Conduct 5–8 moderated usability interviews on a low-fidelity flow.
- Ask participants to compare existing size chart task with FitConfidence prototype.
- Validate the data input burden and comprehension of recommendation explanations.
- Test visual fit simulation expectations and trust risks separately.
