# Problem Statement

## Context
When shopping for apparel online, customers cannot physically try a garment before purchasing. T-Shirt size charts, sizing differences between brands/styles, and model imagery may not fully answer an individual's fit questions.

## Problem
Men shopping for T-Shirts online may be uncertain about which size to select and how the garment's chest, shoulder, sleeve, and length dimensions will translate to their preferred fit. This uncertainty may affect purchase confidence and can contribute to size-related exchanges or returns; the relationship must be validated with product-level data.

## Target segment
Men who shop for T-Shirts online, especially shoppers who report sizing uncertainty or previous fit mismatch. The initial survey includes a small exploratory sample; it does not establish a market-wide segment size.

## Jobs to be done
“When I am choosing a T-Shirt online, help me understand which available size is likely to match my measurements and fit preference, and explain the recommendation so I can decide with greater confidence.”

## Proposed value
- Shopper: more informed size choice, understandable rationale, optional visual fit cue.
- Business (hypothesis): improved recommendation engagement and fit satisfaction; potential impact on returns and customer lifetime value requires testing.

## Goals
- Make the size recommendation process transparent.
- Use product-specific size/garment data.
- Capture fit preference and relevant measurements.
- Provide fallback when data is missing.
- Test whether the experience improves confidence and fit satisfaction.

## Non-goals (initial)
- All apparel categories; start with Men's T-Shirts.
- Medical interpretation of BMI.
- Guaranteed fit prediction or digital twin.
- Virtual try-on/body scanning in the first prototype.
- Production integration with Myntra systems.
