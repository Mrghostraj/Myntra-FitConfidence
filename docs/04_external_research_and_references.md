# External Research & References

This document distinguishes external evidence from our own survey findings. Research is relevant to the problem framing and product design; it does **not** validate FitConfidence specifically.

## Evidence themes

### Online apparel size uncertainty
Balaram, Perdikaki & Galbreth (2022) describe size uncertainty in online apparel and “bracketing” (ordering multiple sizes to keep one) as a consumer behavior that can create return costs. This supports the general problem framing, not a claim about Myntra or our respondents.
- Article: *Bracketing of purchases to manage size uncertainty: Should online retailers be worried?*
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1002/nav.22048

### Fit information on product pages
Researchers studied how online fashion product-page design affects consumers' clothing fit appraisal, highlighting fit information as relevant to garment evaluation.
- Article: *Investigating how online fashion product page design affects the consumer's clothing fit appraisal* (2022)
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1002/cb.2100

### Fit context in reviews
Wang, Ramachandran & Liu Sheng (2021) studied fit opinions and reviewer fit context; the findings suggest that fit valence alone can be limited and that contextual information can help shoppers interpret fit.
- Article: *Do Fit Opinions Matter? The Impact of Fit Context on Online Product Returns*
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1287/isre.2020.0965

### Product page and apparel UX
Baymard's apparel research covers fit/sizing, product imagery, and product-page usability. Treat this as UX research guidance, not independent evidence of the effectiveness of our proposed feature.
- Apparel & Accessories UX research: https://baymard.com/research/apparel-and-accessories
- Product Page UX research: https://baymard.com/research/product-page

### Important counterpoint: size finders are not guaranteed to reduce returns
Patel et al. (2025) analyzed a large high-end fashion e-commerce dataset. Their paper reports that size-finder users were slightly more likely to return items, while repeat-use was associated with higher subsequent customer lifetime value. Context and selection effects matter; this reinforces that “returns reduction” should be a testable hypothesis rather than a guaranteed benefit.
- Article: *Fits like a glove? Knowledge and use of size finders and high-end fashion retail returns*
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jik.2025.100779

### GenAI image generation capability
Official OpenAI developer documentation describes image generation and editing capabilities, including image inputs and generated outputs. The capability to generate an image does not itself establish physical-fit accuracy.
- Image generation guide: https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-generation
- Vision and images guide: https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/images-vision

## Reference-use note
External papers support the general product problem and design considerations. Survey percentages in this project come solely from the project's Google Forms response workbook, analyzed with denominator n=14 eligible recent purchasers. Avoid implying that external research measured Myntra FitConfidence.
